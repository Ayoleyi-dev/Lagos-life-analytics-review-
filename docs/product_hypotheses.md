# Product research brief — hypotheses, not company findings

**17 October 2026 · Independent exploratory review · Not commissioned by Vatar.**

## Evidence snapshot
Six manually coded observations from **one** public r/Nigeria discussion dated 6–10 October. They cannot estimate population sentiment, DAU, retention, churn, revenue, or causal impact. Run `python scripts/quickstart.py` for descriptive counts.

| Question | Public indication | Evidence strength | Proposed validation with authorised Vatar data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does in-game time feel too slow? | OBS-003 compares the clock unfavourably with other simulation games | Very limited: one account | Prototype optional pacing cues; monitor milestone completion and satisfaction |
| Are social-only loops insufficient for repeat engagement? | OBS-006 describes boredom after initial play | Very limited: one account | Diary interviews and measured D1/D7 cohorts; no causal inference without experiments |
| Can release visibility reinforce goodwill? | OBS-004 and OBS-005 praise updates | Very limited: two related remarks in one thread | Track feature adoption and opt-in feedback around releases |
| Are visuals and early gameplay appealing? | OBS-001 and OBS-002 praise play or screenshots | Very limited: one secondhand | Conduct a voluntary survey comparing actual play and screenshots-only viewers |

## Proposed experiments requiring operator approval
**Optional new-player goals:** Test whether a guided first-session checklist improves task completion and next-day return without depressing satisfaction. Randomise consenting newly registered players at player level. Predefine the cohort timezone; measure D1 returns, task completion, session time, client errors and opt-outs. Set sample size from real baseline and desired minimum detectable effect — no invented sample-size claims.

## Prioritised questions
1. Identify what new players regard as a meaningful first milestone.
2. Understand reasons players return after the first day.
3. Measure adoption of announced features rather than equating social comments with successful releases.

Vatar publicly describes jobs, property and social play; virtual economy studies are proposed only, **not** claims about actual revenue or customer lifetime value.
